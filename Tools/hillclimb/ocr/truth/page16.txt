The ship, called the Santa Maria by the Spanish chroniclers and the Gallega by the sailors, was of about a hundred tons, and carried a crew of forty. Of her later history little is known; the ADMIRAL himself, in a letter written from LISBON in the spring, speaks only of her loss on the coast of Hispaniola, and passes on at once to the question of the colony.

Of the three FATES the poet names only one, Clotho, and he describes her not as a spinner but as a weaver; the Moirai of HESIOD are quite different, and the confusion has led more than one commentator astray. It is wise to remember that the popular names of the Fates are late, and that the earliest authors speak of a single power, Moira, without distinction of persons.
The Jesuit MARIANA, in the Historiae de rebus Hispaniae, gives an account of the siege which differs in several particulars from that of the MOORISH chroniclers; where they agree, however, we may feel some confidence that the facts are as stated. The Chronicle of the Cid, which is later, follows MARIANA in the main, but adds a number of details of its own.
The council met in the summer of the year, and its first business was to examine the credentials of the bishops who had come from the distant provinces. Disputes over precedence consumed several days, and it was only after the emperor had sent a letter urging haste that the assembly turned to the question of doctrine. The debates which followed were long and bitter, and the surviving accounts, written for the most part by partisans of one side or the other, can hardly be reconciled with one another.
