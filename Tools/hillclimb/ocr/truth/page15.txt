The ANNALS of Tacitus were written, for the most part, in the principate of TRAJAN, though the exact date is uncertain. The Histories had been composed earlier, and the author himself refers in the Agricola to a work which he intended to write on the reigns of NERVA and Trajan. Whether this plan was ever carried out is not known; nunquam is the word used by some editors, who think the design was abandoned.
In the second edition, published at LONDON in 1788, the author has added a long note on the use of the word constitution, which he now admits to be ambiguous. He had meant by it the whole body of laws and customs; his critics, as he complains, understood only the written instrument. The Federalist had already drawn the distinction, but he seems not to have read it until the book was in print.
The word virtus in the age of CICERO still carries something of its early sense of manly courage, though the philosophers had begun to use it for moral excellence in general. A writer who rendered it everywhere by virtue would give the English reader a false impression; one who rendered it everywhere by courage would give a false impression of another kind. The STOIC usage, which was the commonest in the schools, lies between.

Of her later history little is known; the ADMIRAL himself, in a letter written from LISBON in the spring, speaks only of her loss on the coast of Hispaniola, and passes on at once to the question of the colony.
In the printed catalogue the entry reads: Codex Ambrosianus, a copy of the Iliad on vellum, of the fifth or sixth century, with the pictures of the HOMERIC scenes which give the manuscript its fame. The ILIAD text is fragmentary, but the scholia in the margin, written in a small uncial hand, have preserved many readings that are found nowhere else.
